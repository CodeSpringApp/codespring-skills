# Worked discovery: App Idea Test Drive

Source: Sebastian's iterative naming and image review, 22 September 2026.
This is a decision record, not proof of conversion performance.

## Starting problem

The product researched app ideas and presented demand, buyer pain, existing
spend, competitive openings and revenue-path evidence, with suggested next
steps. The immediate buyer question was whether to build and what to change.
The broader aspiration was a sustainable software business with less risk of
wasting effort. “Validation” felt dull; “one-man SaaS” and “employee-free”
focused on a constraint the product was not selling.

## Discovery sequence

1. Browse business/self-help book titles and covers on Waterstones. Study why
   titles make a result, process or model obvious before reading the book.
2. Explore Solo, habit, psychology, rapid, resilient, quiet cash flow and
   flywheel territories. These were exploratory, not a settled offer.
3. The road-test pattern connected directly to trying an idea before investing
   in building it. Select Idea Test Drive, then App Idea Test Drive for clarity.
4. Bridge the name to the page promise: “Know if your app idea is worth building
   or exactly what to improve.” Research informs this decision; it does not
   guarantee demand, paying customers or a successful business.
5. Make the tool tangible using software/tablet mockups rather than pretending
   to sell the book that inspired the advertising structure.
6. Explore a four-part stack: research tool, case study, pricing matrix and 1:1
   next steps call. Some bonus discussion was tentative; future runs must check
   fulfilment rather than infer inclusion from a rendered image.
7. Simplify the composition into a physically plausible fan, with one app and
   a phone integrated into the stack. Purple replaced disliked green.
8. Create three ad concepts with two execution variants: page outcome,
   mechanism title and test-before-building/idea-to-next-steps explanation.
9. Prefer the single-tablet App Idea Test Drive creative; remove heavy neon
   gradients, sharpen UI and explore a car on a test track as metaphor.
10. Differentiate case study from the call: presentation + small webcam inset
    versus a full-size personal video-call portrait.
11. Create professional founder-led title and $27 variants from the original
    face reference. The $27 version was explicitly selected as very good.
12. Finish with a targeted edit: gradient only on $27 and all text moved upward
    for bottom cropping safety. Keep the portrait and words otherwise stable.

## Reusable decisions versus local choices

Reusable: truthful purchase boundary, familiar action-based mechanism,
product-name/page/ad alignment, tangible digital deliverables, sparse copy,
original identity sources, realistic geometry and precise revisions.

Local: purple palette, $27 price, four-part bundle, Sebastian's portrait,
particular wardrobe and the approved wording. Do not hardcode these into a new
client offer. “Winning” means selected in this design session only.
