# Tangible offer and sparse ad execution

## Product mockup

Represent digital deliverables honestly: software on a device/browser, a video
in a player, a working worksheet, a personal call in a call UI. Do not default
to a physical book because the inspiration advertiser sold a book.

For the session's four-item stack:

- One main app report, with an obvious App Idea Test Drive title.
- Case study as a screen-shared presentation with a small founder webcam tile
  in a bottom corner and playback controls.
- Pricing matrix as a distinct worksheet (confirm its actual pricing utility).
- 1:1 next steps call as a personal video-call view, not just a calendar.

The case-study and call must not look like duplicate large portraits. Avoid two
versions of the main app. A small brand title hidden in a dashboard was not
enough; the buyer should immediately recognise the named offer.

For a fan layout, specify one coherent camera, realistic device aspect ratios,
scale, overlap and shared support plane. A phone should nest within the group's
lower overlap, not dangle below it. Protect all deliverable titles and faces.
Do not stretch a tablet vertically to accommodate every dashboard widget.
Prefer a focused report excerpt to an entire illegible dashboard.

Use true alpha for requested transparent cutouts and inspect it. Finished ads
usually have a deliberate background; do not confuse those with bundle cutouts.

## Three useful ad families

1. **Outcome headline + stack:** exact approved website headline above a clear
   product mockup and one action line.
2. **Named mechanism:** large product title, one short explanatory subheading,
   single hero software device. A route/car metaphor can make test-driving
   tangible, but the software remains the product.
3. **Founder + offer/price:** professional founder portrait, huge offer title,
   optionally a dominant approved price, and one purchase-specific subheading.

Do not add three benefit pills, feature chips, a paragraph, unrelated badges or
filler by habit. Every extra word must resolve a specific confusion. Reuse
competitor hierarchy, not their copy, financial claims, identity or guarantees.

## Founder-led price anchor: selected session execution

Professional dark charcoal studio portrait, founder standing waist-up, natural
eye contact, soft photographic light, credible clothing. Use the original
identity photo; do not derive identity from a previously generated portrait.
The chosen version used a dark overshirt and off-white tee. These are example
styling decisions, not mandatory clothing for every user or campaign.

Over the lower torso, below the face:

1. White condensed bold title: APP IDEA TEST DRIVE.
2. Very large $27 as the visual price anchor.
3. White short subheading: Know if your app idea is worth building or exactly
   what to improve.

No devices or extra CTA were necessary in this selected layout. The earlier
reference's $49/CPA promise was not copied. A proposed virality guarantee was
not included because the research tool does not make apps viral.

Purple was the user's brand direction. Heavy glows and gradients across the
whole composition were rejected. A later explicit exception was a brighter
lavender-to-violet gradient **only inside $27**, making price stand out while
the rest stayed restrained. Latest specific direction wins over broad defaults.

The final edit raised the entire typography group about 8% of canvas height,
leaving roughly 14% bottom clearance. That is a starting point from this 4:5
creative, not a universal platform safe zone. Check the actual placement/crop.
Preserve relative text spacing and never move text into the face.

## Prompt and review discipline

For every input, name its role: original face, real UI, selected composition,
brand palette or competitor hierarchy. Quote exact permitted copy. Specify
what changes and what must remain. Do not promise pixel-identical regeneration.

Example narrow edit brief:

> Preserve portrait, expression, clothing, background, crop and wording. Move
> the entire text group upward by about 8% of canvas height. Change only the
> price fill to a restrained lavender-to-violet gradient. Keep the other text
> white. Add no new objects or copy.

Repeated generated-to-generated edits degraded face likeness, logo lettering
and chart clarity in this session. When this happens, return to original assets
and rebuild the UI with clean typesetting and simple graph strokes. If exact
text/data fidelity is necessary, propose deterministic compositing of original
assets rather than another approximate regeneration, using the available
image tool rules and obtaining any required user choice.

Inspect full resolution and 300–400px feed size: face resemblance, body/hand
continuity, spelling, chart labels, coherent perspective, cropped edges, title
recognition, price emphasis and bottom clearance. Do not call an illustrative
generated report authentic customer evidence. Label example data where needed.
Preserve accepted originals; save versioned files and record the selected one.
