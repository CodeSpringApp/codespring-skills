# Bookshelf research and naming

## Research source and limits

The session used this Waterstones category as a title-browsing starting point:
https://www.waterstones.com/category/non-fiction/economics-finance-business-management/business/ownership-organisation-of-businesses/small-business-self-employment

When packaging this skill on 22 September 2026, automated access to that URL
failed. The examples below come from the user's screenshots and discussion,
not a newly verified category listing. When running new research, browse the
current page or accessible book/publisher pages and record source/date. If
blocked, use supplied covers or other accessible sources and state the limit.
Never imply that a title appeared in a category you could not inspect.

## What to extract

| Session example | Naming lesson | Product-fit caution |
|---|---|---|
| The New Business Road Test | Familiar low-risk trial before committing | Research is not proof of eventual commercial success |
| 48-Hour Start-Up | Concrete task plus bounded time | Do not invent a time-to-result guarantee |
| Built to Sell | Short phrase names a desired business condition | An exit-ready company is not what a validation report delivers |
| Company of One | Instantly graspable operating model | Team size was not the App Idea Test Drive offer |
| The Psychology of Money | Familiar subject plus explanatory lens | Sounds educational, not necessarily an actionable decision tool |
| Start It Up | Lowers perceived entry barrier; simple cover metaphor | Accessible does not mean effortless or risk-free |

Other user-discussed territories included habit, resilient, future-proof,
quiet cash flow, rapid and flywheel. Treat these as brainstorm material rather
than verified book titles or selected mechanisms. Quiet cash flow appealed
emotionally but implied a more distant outcome. MRR flywheel was jargon for a
buyer whose immediate question was whether their idea was worth building.

Separate three layers:

- **Outcome:** a clearer decision about what to build or change.
- **Actual mechanism:** examine demand, pain, existing spend, alternatives and
  possible routes to revenue; identify gaps and next tests.
- **Market-facing name/metaphor:** App Idea Test Drive.

The name won because a buyer already understands test-driving something before
committing. Adding “App” made the object clear. The metaphor also supplied
imagery: route, road test, software dashboard, later a car. The imagery must
still make clear that the buyer receives a software/research offer, not a car,
book or guaranteed successful business.

## Useful working output

For each candidate record: source pattern, candidate name, one-sentence buyer
interpretation, actual fulfilment, implied claim risk and simplest visual.
Reject names needing a paragraph to explain or selling a different outcome.
Avoid forcing the original selection onto unrelated products. A name such as
Solo may be memorable but only fits if independent operation is the real offer.

Test the shortlist aloud as a page/ad bridge:

> [Name] — [what this purchase helps you decide/do]. [Next action].

Only make time, revenue, ease, stability or proof claims when the product and
evidence support them. A book cover is copywriting inspiration, not substantiation.
