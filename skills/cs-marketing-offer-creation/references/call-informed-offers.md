# Call-informed offer workflow

Use when the buyer's problem is already visible in calls or supplied context.
This is a draft method informed by founder offer iteration, not a validated
conversion system. It complements competitor research rather than replacing it.

## 1. Separate the buyer from the technology

Ask what the buyer already sells, what works, which step repeats, what they have
tried, and what they want to buy now. Extract the prospect's words separately
from the seller's pitch. Mark internal motives as inference, not quotes.

- A SaaS owner may want a paid upgrade for existing customers.
- An established agency may want one successful client build to become reusable.
- A beginner may want a first client. That offer attracts a different buyer.

Do not merge these because all three could use the same infrastructure.

## 2. Find the obstacle underneath “save time”

Concrete: similar clients still require similar setup work; improvements to one
agent do not become approved reusable learning; urgent fixes lack a safe testing
path. The buyer may know the desired solution but be spending unpaid time
building the system that would make paid delivery repeatable.

Do not assume maintenance is the main pain if the call says initial setup is
harder. Do not claim a margin ceiling when no margins were disclosed. One
customer's autonomous testing idea may belong in feature research, not the hero.

## 3. Offer one result with one removed obstacle

Draft aspiration and relief separately when useful:

- **Aspiration:** a specific commercial gain without another acquisition burden.
- **Relief:** reuse work already proven in one deployment without repeat setup.

Possible headline shape: “Use this [price] [deliverable] to [result] without
[obstacle].” Keep the mechanism in one supporting sentence. Read it aloud: the
buyer should understand it without learning your architecture first.

Quantification must have a job and a source. A demonstration covering ten
separate client configurations is not proof of ten sales. A hypothetical
additional monthly profit figure needs a model with actual cash costs, new
licence/API/support costs and demand assumptions; saved founder hours alone
are freed capacity. A SaaS founder's revenue is not agency-profit proof.

## 4. Make the deliverable and risk reversal explicit

A paid case study supplies instruction, examples and evidence. It is not a
software licence or done-for-you deployment unless actually included. State
what is bought now and what is a separate back-end purchase. Keep refund scope
and terms clear; a case-study refund is not an earnings guarantee.

For agents, explain approved reusable skills/setup versus separate private
client data. Do not promise zero maintenance, “never breaks” or uncontrolled
self-improvement. Show a reusable foundation feeding client-specific setups,
not a magical chatbot that somehow guarantees profit.

## 5. Reduce, then preserve approval

Return only as many options and detail as requested. Once the user chooses,
retain the exact approved wording in the build handoff. Do not add extra
sections, badges, pricing, guarantees or claims without scope. Match each ad's
promise to its page. Shortening copy must not turn a possible result into an
invented first-person achievement.

## Completion evidence

Deliver a short offer brief with buyer/stage, headline, supporting mechanism,
deliverable, price, CTA, risk reversal and evidence gaps. A simple claim ledger
is enough: claim | source | verified/user-reported/hypothesis | validation needed.
Ask only blocking questions. Hand the approved brief to website or ad-copy work;
publication, spend and implementation remain separately authorised.
