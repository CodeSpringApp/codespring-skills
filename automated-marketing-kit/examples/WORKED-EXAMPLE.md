# Fictional worked example — not a customer result
Everything below is an invented training scenario. QueueLeaf is not a product recommendation. The example.com thread links are placeholders, not real leads. Scores and outcomes illustrate the format; no campaign was run.

## Product brief
QueueLeaf is a fictional beta for 3–15-person design studios. It gives internal purchase requests an owner, due date and approve/reject log. It costs a fictional $12/team/month. It does not sync invoices, move money, support external clients or provide compliance certification. Next step: a demo page supplied by its founder. The founder relationship must be disclosed.

## Research result from supplied fictional source cards
E1: A studio manager says purchase requests get lost in chat and nobody knows who approves them.
E2: A solo freelancer asks for an invoice-chasing app; this is a different task.
E3: An agency needs accounting integration and approval history; QueueLeaf lacks the integration.
These are invented source cards, so a live campaign must replace them with cited public sources. They cannot establish demand, willingness to pay or competitor features.

## Positioning decision
Test small studios already using chat for internal approvals. Position: “Keep internal purchase requests in one queue, with an owner and decision history.” The mechanism is the shared queue, not AI magic. Exclude solo invoice collection and workflows requiring accounting sync. Core objection: “Why not a spreadsheet?” Honest answer: a spreadsheet may be enough at low volume; the queue is relevant when ownership and decision history matter.
