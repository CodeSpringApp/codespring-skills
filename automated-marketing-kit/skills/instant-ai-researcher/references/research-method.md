# Evidence that changes a decision
Start from the task, not your product category. A transcription app may compete with a notebook, a meeting recorder and an assistant; researching only transcription vendors misses the actual alternatives.

## Search matrix
Substitute the buyer's vocabulary, not marketing jargon:
- Problem: `"[task]" "takes hours"`, `"[task]" "keep forgetting"`
- Intent: `"looking for" "[task]"`, `"any tool" "[pain]"`
- Workaround: `"[task]" "spreadsheet"`, `"[task]" "manually"`
- Alternative: `"[competitor]" "alternative"`, `"[competitor]" "missing"`
- Context: `site:reddit.com/r/[community] "[task]"`
Use search/browser tools available in the session; do not invent a connector or presume arbitrary bulk scraping is allowed. Deduplicate copied stories and repeated results. Open full thread context, not only the selected comment. Record the original permalink.

## Comparison discipline
For each meaningful alternative: who uses it, exact job, how it works, price source/date, setup requirements, and a limitation supported by evidence. “No competitor does X” needs broad evidence and should usually be narrowed to “not found in the pages checked”. Never infer a missing feature from one page's silence. Test comparisons with the free/manual alternative too.

## Source confidence
- First-party product docs: useful for stated capability and price; not independent outcome proof.
- Public conversation: useful for this person's stated problem; not market size or willingness to pay.
- Review: useful for a specific experience; verify date/version and possible incentive.
- Founder brief: usable supplied context; distinguish from verified behavior.
- Inference: explicitly label it and say what would confirm or disprove it.

Quote sparingly, preserving meaning and source attribution. Prefer concise paraphrases. Do not copy an entire post. A good insight says “three separate conversations in this sample mention manual copy/paste” and names the sources. A weak one says “everyone hates spreadsheets”.

## Stop/report
A useful bounded first pass might inspect three direct alternatives, two workarounds and six conversations; these are coverage targets, not required counts. If access fails, report the specific gap and continue with accessible sources. Do not evade blocks or claim a live check you could not complete.
