# Reply patterns
Use the thread's actual question. Give the person a useful next step even if they never click. Adapt to their terminology and constraints. Affiliation disclosure belongs next to the recommendation, not hidden in a profile.

Before choosing a pattern, read relevant existing comments and the author's follow-ups. Record what has already been covered and the point your reply adds. Build on the discussion without copying another reply or using other people's promotion as proof of permission.

Choose the mention route deliberately. A directly useful, permitted product recommendation should include the verified link and honest affiliation. A permitted profile reference needs a visible product and a checked destination; flag stale links rather than changing the profile unasked. No-product advice suits tangential fits or restricted promotion. Do not force a fixed ratio, but explain an all-no-mention batch when the campaign's aim is acquisition.

## Specific workaround
“If [observed constraint] is the main issue, first try [concrete step]. Check [failure condition] before [next step]. I build [product], which handles [supported piece], but it won't [relevant limitation]. [Link only if permitted and useful.]”
Use only when the workaround actually fits. Do not claim you tried it without truthful supplied experience.

## Comparing alternatives
“For [their use case], compare [criterion one] and [criterion two]. [Option A] suits [supported condition]; [Option B] may suit [different condition]. I’m the founder of [your option], so factor that in. Ours supports [verified capability] and requires [constraint].”
Do not invent competitor limitations or pose as a neutral customer.

## Clarify a decisive unknown
“Does this need [integration/constraint], or would [simpler approach] work? If [condition], [useful step]; if not, [alternative direction].”
Ask only a question whose answer changes your recommendation. Avoid engagement bait. This pattern often needs no link.

## No-product answer
“The part causing [problem] is likely [evidence-backed mechanism]. Try [specific step] and check [result].”
Use when your product is not appropriate or links are prohibited. Do not add a disguised product reference.

## Reject before posting
Remove generic praise, copied intros, pressure, unsupported superlatives, fake scarcity, fabricated use/customer results and promises that your product cannot support. If the useful part disappears when the pitch is removed, rewrite the answer.
