# Positioning decisions
## Audience comparison
Score each candidate from 0–2 on observed pain, current product fit, reachable public discussion and evidence quality. Do not treat the total as market validation; explain what could reverse the choice. A high-reach audience that cannot use the current product is a poor first campaign.

## Message construction
For [role in situation] who needs [specific task], [product] is a [plain category] that [supported outcome] through [real mechanism]. Compared with [actual alternative], it [relevant difference], with [honest limitation].

Avoid empty distinctions such as “AI-powered”, “all in one”, “revolutionary” or “easier”. Say what changes: “collect invoice approvals in one shared queue” is observable. “Eliminate admin forever” is not.

## Objection map
For each objection: is it a misunderstanding, missing proof, a real product limitation or a price/access mismatch? Reply with the corresponding fact, evidence, candid limitation or narrower target audience. Do not write copy around a product limitation as if it were solved.

## Test one idea
Choose one target audience and one primary next step. Define what qualitative feedback would support the message and what would contradict it. A small response sample cannot establish a conversion lift. Keep competitors' names out of claims you cannot substantiate.
